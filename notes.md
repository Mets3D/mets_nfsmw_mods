- 2026-10-09: **Pause > Options > Rumble** (`nfsmw_camera_settings` 0.13 with `nfsmw_controller_support` 0.6). It is built like the Camera page: the Player category's screen, its rows replaced.
  - **Row 1: the game's own Vibration setting.** That is `PlayerSettings`+4, Rumble. `cFrontendDatabase::GetPlayerSettings` (0x50E460) returns options settings +0x80 + 0x14 × player. The strings are the game's own: "Vibration" 0xDDDC5E1B (the consoles' `COVibration` row; that class is not in the PC exe), "On" 0x417B2604, "Off" 0x70DFE5C2.
  - **Other rows: one per rumble effect.** Each has a level of 0-100: how hard the controller shakes at that effect's strongest moment. Both motors run at the same level, so the same number means the same strength on every row. Steps are 5, or 1 with Shift.
  - **Changing a level plays a sample.** Nothing else drives the motors while the game is paused, so `nfsmw_controller_support`'s rumble thread plays it.
  - **Accept, Back and Defaults work as on the Camera page.** Defaults also turns Vibration back on.
  - **The levels belong to `nfsmw_controller_support`.** They are stored in `nfsmw_controller_support.ini [rumble]`, and `nfsmw_controller_support.asi` exports `nfsmw_pad_rumble_count/label/help/get/default/set/preview/save`. `nfsmw_camera_settings` finds them with `GetModuleHandle("nfsmw_controller_support.asi")`; without them there is no Rumble entry.
  - **Fixes after the first test of the page:**
    - **Vibration Off still rumbled.** The PC build's `CanDoFFB` (0x6EE400) calls GetSettings but drops the result: it only checks `bRumbleEnabled`. So Vibration Off stopped collisions (`OnCollision` does check it) but nothing else. The DoFFB hook now reads `PlayerSettings`+4 itself, through GetSettings (method 5 of the interface at +0x4C in DoFFB's view).
    - **Shift kick:** upshifts only.
    - **Impacts and Scrapes merged** into Collisions. Scraping grinds at half the level.
    - **Levels now mean an ordinary instance.** They used to mean the effect's strongest possible case. Typical play rarely got near that, so the menu sample, which played the strongest case, felt much stronger than the game. Calibrated on the logged values: drift full at 9 m/s sideways, wheelspin at 12 m/s, collisions at a 6 m/s speed change, rough ground at 15 m/s. The railroad surface has no RenderNoise but a WheelEffectIntensity of 100, so that is used as its roughness. The rev limiter needs 0.6 s at max revs, because the automatic gearbox sits there for about 0.5 s before each upshift.
    - The 2-second log line now lists each effect's share.
    - **Races felt weaker and later than the menu samples.** A full-speed wall hit felt like a slow 1.5 s ramp and much weaker than its sample, although the log shows 100% set at the moment of impact. In a race the game thread sent a command every frame the level changed; the samples went out from a separate thread on a steady schedule. Now only the rumble thread talks to the controller, checking every 10 ms:
      - the race just sets the level it should play;
      - a change goes out at most every 30 ms, but a rise of 10% or more, and stopping, go out at once;
      - a steady level is re-sent every 0.5 s.

      For diagnosis, every send is logged for 2.5 s after the first big jolts and the first previews, along with how many updates per second the game makes. Big jolts also stay at full strength longer (0.05 + 0.15 × severity s).
    - **Second timing bug, found with counters in the log:** `pad_in_use` stored `last_active = now | 1`, which can be one tick ahead of the clock (GetTickCount ticks every ~15 ms, so several frames share a tick). A check in the same tick then computed `now - last_active` = 0xFFFFFFFF and decided the pad had been idle for weeks, cutting 25-45% of frames. Fixed with a separate flag and no `| 1`. Lesson: never store a time in a variable another comparison treats as "not ahead of now"; compare differences as signed. The "hold zero levels for 120 ms" workaround that this replaced was removed. NOS also used to fade to 45% of its level; it now holds about 85% for as long as it lasts.
    - **The first timing bug, in the rumble thread:** The trace showed a 0% sent every 60-80 ms between the real values. The rumble thread computed `GetTickCount() - g_last_rumble_update`, and when the game thread stored a time one tick newer than the thread's clock reading, the unsigned difference wrapped to about 4 billion ms. The thread then took the game for stalled and stopped the motors. The earlier watchdog had the same bug. Fixed by reading the shared time first and comparing signed. The game feeds DoFFB about 110-140 times per second, so it runs per sim step, not per frame.
  - **Icon:** `OPTIONS_RUMBLE` from the texture pack. `set_mods.ps1` now packs every `camera_settings/OPTIONS_*.png`. Without that texture the Controller entry's icon (0xA04A7B26) is used.
- 2026-10-09: `nfsmw_controller_support` 0.5: **rumble**. Correction to the entry below: the game does get a feedback object for the PC pad. `GameDevice` (ctor 0x634B50) contains an `IFeedback` at +0x2C (vtable 0x8A7BD4) whose methods are all empty except PauseEffects (0x628800) and ResetEffects (which calls Pause). So a pad never rumbles. `LocalPlayer::SetControllerPort` (0x6F22E0, thiscall `(port)`, `ret 4`; this view: feedback +0xC, port +0x18) is hooked: after it, our object takes the feedback slot and passes every call on to the game's object. Call order per frame, from `DoFFB` (0x6EE490; decomp `LocalPlayer.cpp`):
  - `CanDoFFB` (profile Rumble setting = `PlayerSettings`+4, on by default; `bRumbleEnabled` int at 0x8F86E0 = 1; input not paused; not under AI control; not animating) or `PauseEffects` only;
  - Resume, Begin;
  - per wheel: TireSlip (|current slip|, m/s), TireSkid (|lateral speed|, m/s), RoadNoise (absolute speed, m/s), each with the wheel's surface (the `null` surface in the air, 0xD929E923 with a blown tire);
  - RPM(powerband 0..1, overrev always 0, throttle), ShiftPotential, NOS(engaged, capacity) if the car has NOS, EngineBlown if it has `IEngineDamage` or else Shifting;
  - End.

  Catch found in the first test: if `mWheelDevice` (+0x5C in DoFFB's view, +0x10 in SetControllerPort's) is set and `IsConnected`, DoFFB calls the wheel's `UpdateForces` and returns without touching the feedback object.
  - `SteeringWheelDevice`: vtable 0x8A7A30, UpdateForces 0x6430D0 (DirectInput effects).
  - Its `IsConnected` (0x628E10) only asks whether the port has a DirectInput joystick, so an Xbox pad counts as a wheel.
  - Fix: `LocalPlayer::DoFFB` is hooked to hide the wheel during the call when all DirectInput joysticks seen are XInput ones. It is left alone elsewhere because the steering model asks it for the steering type (`SuspensionRacer::CalculateMaxSteering`).

  Collisions arrive through `OnCollision` (0x6F2370, same Rumble checks). They reached the plugin in the first test; the speed change separates walls and cars (2-13 m/s) from props (0.5-1 m/s at 21 m/s closing speed).

  Measured in the second test.
  - Surface data (RenderNoise; WheelEffect frequency/intensity):

    | Surface | RenderNoise | WheelEffect |
    |---|---|---|
    | `asphalt`, `asphalt_no_leaves`, `concrete`, `null` | none | 0/0 |
    | `wood` | 4 Hz, 1°, full at 12 m/s | 120/100 |
    | `grass` | 10 Hz, 1°, full at 50 m/s | 40/80 |
    | `gravel` | 10 Hz, 1°, full at 50 m/s | 60/110 |

  - Tire skid (lateral speed) is 2-5 m/s in ordinary corners and 8-11 in drifts. Wheelspin is 5-10 m/s at a launch and 34 in a burnout. Cruising revs sit at 0.7-0.9 of the powerband, so the engine hum was a constant 6-12% on the heavy motor, which hid the events. Since then every effect has its own `[rumble]` multiplier, reloaded live when the ini changes.

  Third test, fixes:
  - **Gear changes were never reported.** DoFFB calls `UpdateShifting` only for cars without `IEngineDamage`, and the player's car has one, so it gets `UpdateEngineBlown` instead. The DoFFB hook now reads the gear itself:
    - GetSimable is method 3 of the IEntity at +0x2C in DoFFB's view.
    - `QueryInterface` is 0x5D59F0: thiscall on the interface's object (+4), takes the handle, `ret 4`.
    - The `ITransmission` handle is 0x404010. Its methods are GetGear 1, IsGearChanging 4, GetShiftPotential 11.
    - `GearID`: 0 reverse, 1 neutral, 2 first, and so on.
  - **Steady levels need re-sending.** The pad is an Xbox Wireless Controller on Bluetooth (path `HID#{00001812-...}&VID_045E&PID_0B20`). A steady level seemed to stop, which matches what I remember of Bluetooth Xbox pads dropping a rumble after a few seconds. The motors are now re-sent every 0.5 s.
  - **Blown tire surface.** It is the surface whose collection key (+0x20 of the `Attrib::Collection` at instance +4) is 0xD929E923.
  - **Light motor only, chattering, felt weak.** The rev limiter used only the light motor, switched on and off at 12 Hz, and was subtle even at ×100 (log: light motor at 100%). Two reasons:
    - The light motor is much weaker than the heavy one.
    - On/off chatter that fast blurs: motor spin-up plus Bluetooth latency.

    Now effects use both motors with smooth pulses of at most 8 Hz, and a level past one motor's maximum spills half its excess into the other motor.
  - **IEngineDamage covers two cases.** It is the drag race overheating engine and the opening race's sabotaged car (`IsSabotaged`). Spike strips blow tires, not engines. `Sim::Collision::Info` layout:
  - type bits 0-2 at +0x1C (1 object, 2 world, 3 ground); sliding is bit 7;
  - closingVel +0x20, force +0x2C;
  - impulseA +0x5C / impulseB +0x6C = each body's change of speed in m/s (force × 1/mass);
  - slidingVel +0x70.

  Surface layout data: `RenderNoise` {Hz, degrees, min speed, max speed} at +0xCC (the renderer's body shake, `CarRenderConn::AddRoadNoise`), name +0xDC, `WheelEffectFrequency` u16 +0xF8, `WheelEffectIntensity` u8 +0xFA. The non-layout `FFB_ROLL` / `FFB_SKID` / `FFB_SLIP` records are not read. The decomp's `Misc/Rumble.cpp` holds an older envelope-based rumble (crash envelopes 0.8 s and 1.2 s long), used as a reference for strengths. The plugin rumbles only while the pad was used in the last 30 s and the game has focus, and a watchdog thread stops the motors 250 ms after the game stops updating. Settings: `nfsmw_controller_support.ini [rumble]`.
