# The racing AI (AIVehicleHuman / AIGoalRacer) and our use of it

Decomp sources: `Tools/nfsmw-decomp/src/Speed/Indep/Src/AI/` (`Common/AIVehicle.cpp`, `Actions/AIActionRace.cpp`, `Actions/AIActionGetUnstuck.cpp`, `Common/AIGoal.cpp`) and `World/Common/WRoadNetwork.cpp`, `WPathFinder.cpp`. Marks: [read] [tested] [guess].

## Words used here (one word per thing; the game's own word where it has one)
- **Node, segment, road**: the road network's three element kinds (`WRoadNode`, `WRoadSegment`, `WRoad`: a run of segments with one id). A segment joins two nodes.
- **Profile**: the cross-section a node points at (`WRoadProfile`, shared by many nodes). **Lane**: one strip of a profile across the road (`WRoadLane`; the game counts them as `fNumZones`). A lane has a **type** (`ELaneType` value: 1 traffic lane, 2 median or shoulder, 7 and 13 not drivable for racing).
- **Nav**: a `WRoadNav`, the game's object that keeps a position on the road network: the segment it is on, how far along it, its lane offset across the road, and (when it has one) a path. Every AI car has several; "the car's nav" below is the one it steers by (the drive-to nav), which is not at the car: it is a look-ahead target 30 to 100 m (by speed, `AiNavLookAheadTable`) ahead of it on the path. Other things use navs too (the race route, the GPS, traffic, cops). A nav also has a **nav lane type** (`ELaneType` again: racing by default), which selects the mask of lane types it may drive on (`drivable_lanes`).
- **Path**: the list of segments the route search (`AStarSearch`, A*) found for a nav. In our code, the ini and the overlay this is called the **route** (cyan). Not to be confused with the game's "race route", a path type used for the race's own course.
- **Cookie trail**: the nav's steering corridor ahead of the car, a row of cross-sections (`NavCookie`), each with a left and a right limit. We say **corridor** for it (green in the overlay). The road inspector draws a segment as a **road shape** (its left and right side and its lanes), which is not the cookie trail.
- **Active / hovered** (road inspector only): the node or segment you clicked / the one under the cross in the middle of the picture.

## Turning it on for a player [read; tested in split screen: see below]
- ExtraOptions' F6 calls `Game_ForceAIControl(int)` 0x6123B0 / `Game_ClearAIControl` 0x612420 (cdecl): argument 0 = first local player, any other = the last entry of the local player list (0x92D87C, count 0x92D884). They find the player's IHumanAI (handle 0x403770) and call vtable +0x10 `SetAiControl(bool)` (`AIVehicleHuman::SetAiControl` 0x4291C0: goal `AIGoalRacer`, `EEnableAIPhysics` event); +0xC is `GetAiControl`. Game state 0x925E90 == 6 is in-game.
- `nfsmw_improved_ai_opponents` (`Mods/improved_ai_opponents/src/ai_control.cpp`; it needs `nfsmw_split_screen_support` for its per-frame tick and its draw hook: `common/split_screen_api.h`) does it for the players about 2 s into a race: each player's settings (`settings.cpp`; the ini sections `[player_1]` / `[player_2]` and the page Options > AI Driving, `menu.cpp`, a kNewEntry page of `nfsmw_custom_menus` whose player is `UIOptionsScreen::PlayerToEdit` 0x91CA24): control = off | vanilla | improved (a human drives with the overlay on = the improved AI plans its route beside the car), skill, nitro, shortcuts, catch-up, overlay. Every frame of a race compares the control setting with what was applied (`apply_control`), so a change takes effect at once. F7 flips player 1's control between off and improved, F9 plans the route again. Every change we make to the AI is per car: a stub asks which player's AI (the action's `IVehicleAI` at `AIAction` +0x40) it is, and the "vanilla" cars and the opponents get the game's behaviour.

## What the AI is [read]
- `AIVehicleHuman::GetSkill` 0x435190 returns 1.0, `GetCatchupCheat` 0x4351B0 returns 0, whatever the race settings. Opponents' own skill (`AIPerpVehicle::ComputeSkill`, only for non-players): Quick Race difficulty 0.15 / 0.4 / 1.0 (0.8 with catch-up off); career: the character's `SkillLevel` * 0.01 + the adaptive difficulty. Catch-up glue is only added for non-player cars.
- `AIGoalRacer` = actions `AIActionRace` + `AIActionGetUnstuck`. Route: `WRoadNav::FindPath` (A*, cost = segment length only, no surface or speed penalty) to the next checkpoint. Marked shortcuts: a human-class car (`kPathPlayer`) may only use the shortcut whose number its nav has; a racer (`kPathRacer`) rolls per shortcut with chance min + skill * (max - min) from the race's shortcut marker.
- Steering target: `WRoadNav::UpdateOccludedPosition` builds a corridor of road cross-sections ("cookie trail", `NavCookie` Left / Right / Centre) and punches holes for avoidables (`HolePunchAvoidables`: at most 32 bodies, only those that would be reached within 3 s); the car aims at the farthest point visible through it. `AIVehicle::OnSteering` steers to `mDest`. Nothing brakes for traffic: the speed limit comes from the curvature of the trail (`GetPotentialSpeed`) and skill.
- AI steering vs a human's [read in the decomp; the hooks written, not yet run]: `SuspensionRacer::DoSteering` 0x69E9E0 uses `DoHumanSteering` 0x69E820 when `IHumanAI::IsPlayerSteering` (`AIVehicleHuman`, 0x435240: not under AI control, not drag steering) and `DoAISteering` otherwise: the AI's wheel goes to `ABSOLUTE_MAX_STEERING` (45 degrees) x the tires' steering x the input at once, at any speed; a human's goes through `CalculateMaxSteering` 0x68E9E0 (range by forward speed, throttle and brake, how long the input was held, tunings, after a collision) and `CalculateSteeringSpeed` 0x68EC70 (rate limit by speed and by how fast the input moves). The AI divides its steering angle by `ISuspension::GetMaxSteering` 0x6A9700 (`mSteering.Maximum`, suspension + 0x128; only `DoAISteering` writes it), so with human steering it would ask for angles it cannot get. Our Super Steer setting off (`ai_control.cpp`): the `DoSteering` hook makes `IsPlayerSteering` true for an improved player's car under AI control and afterwards sets `Maximum` to `LastMaximum` (+ 0x12C) x tires' steering (tires pointer + 0xA0, STEERING + 0x48), at most 45. Everything else of the car's handling is the same for the AI; the catch-up cheats (`ICheater::GetCatchupCheat`) are 0 for a player's AI unless Catch-Up is on.
- Stuck: `AIActionGetUnstuck::CanBeAttempted` 0x4115C0: gas >= 0.5 for 3.0 s with less than 3 m moved, then reverse for 2 s (the timer and the distance share the constant 3.0 at 0x890604).
- Nitrous: `AIActionRace::Update` 0x428720 only when `!GetOwner()->IsPlayer()` (the `jne` at 0x42905E skips it); the rest is `GetPotentialNOS` 0x407BE0 and the gaps in `AIActionRace.cpp`.

## The corridor ("cookie trail") and avoidables [read; PC offsets from the exe, not run]
- `WRoadNav::IncNavPosition` moves the nav's head (`fPosition`) ahead by the look-ahead (30..100 m by speed) and `UpdateCookieTrail` 0x76EC70 records a `NavCookie` every `cookie_gap` = clamp(look-ahead / 26, 1, 3) m: a road cross-section with `Left` / `Right` (x, z of the road's edges from the nav's left / right splines), `Forward` (2D), `LeftOffset` / `RightOffset` (signed distances of the edges from `Centre` across `Forward`), `Curvature`, `Length` (since the previous cookie), `Centre` (x, y, z) and the segment it is on. The trail is a ring of 32 (about 37..96 m). `CookieTrail<NavCookie, 32>`: `mCount` +0, `mLast` +4, `mCapacity` +8, data at +0x10, 0x40 bytes each (`NthOldest(n)` = data[count < 32 ? n : (last + 1 + n) % 32]). The PC `NavCookie` has the decomp's layout (Left 0, Right 8, Forward 0x10, Length 0x18, Curvature 0x1C, LeftOffset 0x20, RightOffset 0x24, Flags 0x28, Centre 0x30, SegmentParameter 0x3C, segment number 0x3E).
- PC `WRoadNav` offsets (the decomp's are 0xC higher from `fNavType` on, and differ before) [read]: `pCookieTrail` +8, `bCookieTrail` +0x55, `nRoadOcclusion` +0x58, `nAvoidableOcclusion` +0x5C, `fPosition` +0x98, `fApexPosition` +0x158, `fOccludedPosition` +0x164 (`GetOccludedPosition` 0x4106D0 returns it when occluded, else `fPosition`), `fNavType` +0x78, `fSegmentInd` +0x8E, `fSegTime` +0x90.
- How wide the corridor is: for the racing lane the road profile's drivable lanes (`IsDrivable(lane type)`) from the leftmost to the rightmost contiguous one, with the nav position kept `fVehicleHalfWidth + 1.5` m inside (`SnapToSelectableLane`). So whatever the profile calls drivable (a dirt shoulder, a median) is road to the AI.
- Avoidables: only `AIVehicle` is an `AIAvoidable` (cars; no pillars, props or other scenery). `AvoidableManager` updates a sweep-and-prune grid every 0.25 s (position, radius = max(avoidable radius, 2 * speed)); the overlapping ones are an AI's `GetAvoidableList` (its neighbours). `UpdateOccludedPosition` 0x776600 works on a local copy of the trail (so the stored trail is always the uncut road): `HolePunchAvoidables` 0x775BE0 takes up to 32 bodies, skips those behind or more than 3 s from closest approach, predicts the point of impact, finds the closest cookie to it and `CookieCutter` pulls the left or right edge of that cookie and the following ones in (to pass on the side where it fits, else the side the car is already on) with a margin of half the car's width + a closeness factor; cut cookies get `Flags` |= 1 and their centres are re-centred.
- The steering target is a funnel ("string pulling") over the copy: from the car, left and right limit rays are narrowed cookie by cookie; the first cookie whose centre falls outside is the occluder; its limit point is the apex (`fApexPosition`, flag 1 = cut by an avoidable, else the road's own edge). Occluded: the target is placed on the near side of the apex (`desired = apex + apex_to_nav * dist_to_apex`, offset at most the apex's width, scaled down by the closing speed when a car is the occluder); not occluded: the target is the nav head `fPosition`. `CookieTrailCurvature` 0x778910 (the speed limit's input) is the larger of the apex angle / width and the length-weighted mean of the cookies' curvature (clamped to +-0.01).
- Not in the trail: static objects. Pillars and props are not avoided by this system at all.

## The road network: what is stored where [read in the decomp (`WRoadElem.h`, `WRoadNetwork.cpp`, `WPathFinder.cpp`) and checked against the PC sizes; not run]
- Five flat arrays in the track's map data (group `RN`: header `hd` with the counts, `pf` profiles, `nd` nodes, `sg` segments, `rd` roads), pointed at from [0x9B38B8..0x9B38C8]; `WRoadNetwork` is a sim subsystem (`BIND_SIM_SUBSYSTEM`: `Init` / `Shutdown`), so barriers and shortcuts are probably worked out again whenever a race loads [guess: from the subsystem binding].
- **Node** (`WRoadNode`, 0x20): position (x, y, z), its index, a **profile index**, a count and up to **7 segment indices**. That is all: no width, no direction. Connections between nodes are the segments.
- **Segment** (`WRoadSegment`, 0x16): the link between two nodes (`fNodeIndex[2]`), its length (`nLength` * 1000 / 65535 m), the road it belongs to (`fRoadID`, -1 at junctions), `fFlags` (below), and for each end a **handle**: a 3 byte direction (`vStartHandle`, `vEndHandle`) and a 16 bit length. The handle gives the road's direction at that end (the cross-section at the end is perpendicular to it: `GetRightVec`) and, when the Curved flag (bit 8) is set, the control points of the cubic spline the road follows (`BuildSegmentSpline`: node + handle). Without the flag the road is a straight line between the nodes. So a node shared by several segments has one cross-section per segment, with that segment's direction.
- **Profile** (`WRoadProfile`, 0x40, `pf` array, shared: many nodes use one): up to 15 lanes across the road (the game calls the count and the middle `fNumZones` and `fMiddleZone`), each `{type (4 bits), width (14 bit signed * 100 / 8191 m), offset from the centre (same encoding)}`, and `fMiddleZone`, the lane where the opposite direction starts (forward lanes are the lanes from it on; the segment's StartInverted / EndInverted flags (bits 10, 9) flip that at an end). A racing car may drive every type except 7 and 13 (`drivable_lanes[kLaneRacing]` = 0xFFFFDF7F). A node's width is its profile's, the width along a segment is the two ends' interpolated.
- **Road** (`WRoad`, 8 bytes): a run of segments with one id: a shortcut number (0xFF = none), minimum width (`nMinWidth`), speech id.
- **Segment flags** (`fFlags`): bit 0 decision (short connector at a junction; the route search never takes two in a row, except for cops), 1 no traffic, 2 race route forward, 3 intersection, 4 entrance, 5 cops xor traffic, 6 one way, 7 shortcut, 8 curved, 9 / 10 end / start profile inverted, 11 chopper stays low, 12 crosses a barrier, 13 crosses a player-only barrier, 14 lane map, 15 in race.
- **Direction** is three things. (a) A segment's own orientation, node 0 to node 1, with the profile inversion; fixed in the map. (b) **One way** (bit 6): every driver may only go node 0 to node 1; fixed in the map. (c) **In race** and **race route forward** (bits 15, 2): per race; `WRoadNetwork::AddRaceSegments(nav)` sets them on the segments of the route a nav found for the race (path type `kPathRaceRoute`), `FlagSegmentRaceDirection` the direction of each from the next segment; `ResetRaceSegments` clears them. They are used by navs with the race filter (snapping to the race's roads) and by wrong-way detection; the AI's route search ignores them. Traffic cars use the profile's forward / backward lanes, not the race flags.
- **Which roads are outside the race** are not nodes but segments that cross a barrier: `ResolveBarriers` (at `WRoadNetwork::Init`) takes every enabled `TrackPathBarrier` of the track and flags (bit 12; bit 13 when the barrier is the player-only kind) the segments whose spline crosses it, except the roads at the race's barrier exemption markers. `Admissible` rejects flagged segments for racers, players, GPS and the race route.
- **Shortcuts are per race**: `ResolveShortcuts` (at `Init`) gives each shortcut marker of the loaded race (a position, a direction and a min / max chance) the road under it (`InitAtPoint`): `WRoad.nShortcut` = the marker's number, and every segment of that road gets the shortcut flag. The same road is a shortcut in one race and ordinary in another (or not exist as one). A racer's search takes a shortcut when `MakeShortcutDecision` rolls it (chance = min + skill * (max - min), once per shortcut and search); a player-type nav only the one it is on (see below).
- **The route search** (`AStarSearch`): the states are (road node, segment it was reached by), the edges the node's segments; cost = the sum of the segments' lengths, heuristic = the straight distance to the goal, so it takes the shortest way and nothing else counts (not width, surface, curvature or speed). It is planned to the next checkpoint (not the whole race) and again only when the nav is off its path (or F9). `Admissible` per `EPathType` is above.
- **The corridor's width and shape** come from the profile and handles, computed by the nav while it moves (`SetBoundPos`, `RebuildSplines`, `EvaluateSplines`, `UpdateCookieTrail`): at each end of the current segment it takes the lane nearest to the nav's lane offset that a racing car may drive on and widens it over the neighbouring lanes that are also drivable (types other than 7 and 13); the limits are the outer edges of that run, pulled in by the vehicle's half width + 1.5 m on each side (`how_unsafe` widens it back if less than that is left). The left and right limit points of the two ends are joined as a line, or as two splines (with the same handles scaled) for a curved segment; a cookie is the cross-section at a point along that. So the corridor is **not** the road centred: it follows the profile's lane offsets, and a median or shoulder of a drivable type is inside it. Anything that is not in the network (pillars, props, walls) is never in the corridor's way unless the profile marks that space as type 7 or 13 or leaves it out of the zones.
- Our F8 view (`overlay.cpp`) draws what the AI is calculating: the segments of the route it has planned (`route_of`: from the car's own segment to the goal: the path segment nearest the car, among the 12 before the nav's, drawn only from the car on) in full, as the road inspector draws an active segment (`segment_draw::add_lanes` and an orange outline), the steering target and the apex. F11 is the road inspector, an overview. With neither on nothing is drawn. The earlier overlay's cyan route line, "roads not taken" and orange alternative corridors are gone.

## The route search weighs the road for the improved AI (`path_cost.cpp`) [read; not yet tested in the game]
- `AStarSearch::Service` 0x788040 costs a step as `parent cost + segment length` (stored as `unsigned short` in 0.25 m). A stub at the `fmul [0x9B3A14]` that makes the length (0x788206; edi = the search, ebx = the current `AStarNode`, ebp = the candidate segment) adds an extra for searches whose nav (`AStarSearch` +0x2C) is a modified player's drive-to nav. `AStarNode`: parent slot +8 (short, -1 for the two seeds), segment +0xA, road node +0xC, cost +0xE. The heuristic stays the straight distance, so it holds.
- Road width: profile lane types are 4-bit; for a racing car types in `0xFFFFDF7F` are drivable (all but 7 and 13). Seen so far: type 1 = 4 m lanes, type 2 = 0.5..6 m (shoulders / medians). Highway segments 39..41 m, mid roads 19 m, connectors 13 m; `WRoad` +5 (`nMinWidth`) 255 / 161 / 110 on the sample; junction segments have road id -1.
- **The improved AI's route search costs time** (`path_cost.cpp`, the stub at 0x788206; replaces the earlier width and turn terms, which were gated off below 3 m/s and measured angles between straight node-to-node lines) [written; not yet run]: a step costs (seconds * top_speed) metres, so a step at the top speed costs its length and the straight-distance heuristic stays valid. A segment's seconds come from `drive_segment`: the entry speed (the car's own for the first steps of a search, else the speed the cheapest route to that (node, segment) left its last segment at: `g_states`, an approximation), the segment's limit speed (`geometry::corner_limit`, worked out for every segment in a thread of its own at the first search), braking for a limit below the entry speed (the extra time the braking takes over the distance at the entry speed), then acceleration along the segment (`accel` less nearer `top_speed`) up to the limit. The car's numbers come from `car_stats.cpp` (generic ones, `kDefault...`, when the game's cannot be read); `[debug] log` shows (each search's start and each step's entry speed, limit, exit speed and time). The route is also written to the log whenever it changes (`route #n:`: segments with length and limit speed, and the time at those speeds).
- **No kink at junctions** [seen in the road inspector by the user, not measured by code]: the segments that meet at a node leave it in the direction the other arrives, so the route cost has no turn term between segments; each segment's own curve (`corner_limit`) is all the corner there is.

## The route search: what it uses and when it runs [read in the decomp and the exe; the F9 re-plan and the human nav's search not yet tested in the game]
- Road segment flags (`WRoadSegment` +0xA): bit 0 decision, 1 no traffic, 2 race route forward, 3 intersection, 4 entrance, 5 cops xor traffic, 6 one way (drivable node 0 to node 1 only), 7 shortcut, 8 curved (a segment has start and end control handles: it is a spline, the F8 view draws chords), 11 chopper stay low, 12 crosses a barrier, 13 crosses a player-only barrier, 14 lane map, 15 in race.
- `AStarSearch::Admissible` 0x773B60 (what the search may use, by `EPathType`): a one-way segment against its direction, a segment that crosses a barrier (bits 12 and 13: `WRoadNetwork::ResolveBarriers` sets them for the track path barriers of the loaded race, so they are the roads that leave the race's part of the map), and a shortcut segment unless allowed. It never looks at the in-race flag. Cops (`kPathCop`) only skip one-way. For a human-class car (`kPathPlayer`: `DRIVER_HUMAN` and `DRIVER_REMOTE`, what the AI drives as) a shortcut is allowed only when its number equals `WRoadNav::GetShortcutNumber()` 0x76FDE0 of the nav (the shortcut it is on, or one attached to the decision segment it is at): the search never plans a shortcut from afar. In the exe the player case compares the numbers in `Admissible` itself, it does not call `WRoadNav::MakeShortcutDecision` 0x76FF00 (only the racer case, type 2, does; there it rolls `min + skill * (max - min)` of the race's shortcut marker) [read in the exe; a hook on MakeShortcutDecision alone had no effect on the AI]. Our hook on `Admissible` (`path_cost.cpp`) rejects shortcut segments for an improved car set to Avoid and evaluates them with the racer rule for Allowed / Always, and the MakeShortcutDecision hook then says yes.
- The in-race flag (bit 15) is an output: `WRoadNetwork::AddRaceSegments` flags the segments of a path a nav found (the race route) when the race loads; navs with the race filter only snap to such segments (`InitAtPoint`), and wrong-way detection uses it. The AI's route search ignores it.
- When it runs: `AIActionRace` (and `AIVehicleHuman::Update` for a human-driven car, during a race) calls `WRoadNav::FindPath` only while the drive-to nav is not on a path (type 3) and not already searching: the route is planned once and then followed. `WRoadNav::CancelPathFinding` 0x770060 drops the path (type back to direction) and the next frame plans again: that is F9. So the route of a car that is not re-planned stays what it was, wherever the car goes. A search is spread over frames (`PathFinder::Submit`, `AStarSearch::Service`).
- The road data is sparse in places (a curved piece with a few nodes; the area with the pillars has two ways through in the graph, while a player has many): the search chooses between links only at nodes where roads branch, within a link the car steers in the corridor. Missing links cannot be fixed by costs.

## Making the AI of a player's car race like an opponent [read in the decomp and the exe; not yet tested in the game]
The AI of a player's car (`AIVehicleHuman`, under AI control it runs the same `AIVehicleRacecar::Update` as an opponent) differs from an opponent (DRIVER_RACER) in: the skill (`AIVehicleHuman::GetSkill` 0x435190 is a constant 1.0; an opponent's is `AIPerpVehicle::GetSkill` 0x409340 = base skill + glue skill, the base from `ComputeSkill`: Quick Race 0.15 / 0.4 / 1.0 by difficulty, 0.8 instead of 1.0 when the race has no catch-up, career from the racer; it is 0 for a player), the catch-up (`AIVehicleHuman::GetCatchupCheat` 0x4351B0 is 0; `AIPerpVehicle::Update` computes the glue skill with `GRaceStatus::ComputeCatchUpSkill` only when `!GetOwner()->IsPlayer()` (the `jne` at 0x429501) and a racer info exists, and `GetCatchupCheat` 0x409390 = clamp(base + glue - 1) * CatchupCheatTable; `AiCatchupAcceleration` turns it into 1.0..2.0 times the acceleration), nitrous (`AIActionRace::Update` skips it for a player: the `jne` at 0x42905E), the nav's path type (`kPathPlayer` 4 against `kPathRacer` 2: the shortcut rule, `Admissible` / `MakeShortcutDecision`) and the racer info (`pRacerInfo`, which `IsRacing()` needs: it also turns on the nav's race filter every frame). What is left is the car being a player's car (physics: head-on collision damping, collision with the world, shifting; `EEnableAIPhysics` only seems to touch sound).
- Offsets seen from the IVehicleAI pointer of an `AIPerpVehicle` (the object is 0x4C before it): base skill +0x75C, glue skill +0x760, racer info +0x758 (the object's +0x7A4: the decomp's offsets minus 0x20); the nav's path type `WRoadNav` +0x7C.
- `ai_control.cpp` every frame, for a car whose control is vanilla or improved: writes the skill setting to the base skill, fills the racer info from `GRaceStatus::GetRacerInfo(ISimable *)` 0x5E8B20 (object at [0x91E000]) when it is null, and sets the nav's path type to racer for the vanilla AI (back to player otherwise); `GetSkill` / `GetCatchupCheat` of the player's AI jump to the base class's with the catch-up setting on (0 with it off); the `jne` at 0x429501 goes through a stub that lets a car whose catch-up is on run the glue code; the nitrous stub lets a vanilla car through. The improved AI keeps the player path type (our `Admissible` / `MakeShortcutDecision` hooks) and has the nitro and shortcut settings.
- Nitrous "Fair" is the game's rule: `AIActionRace`'s start-up sets `mNosCapability` (PC +0x8C) and `mUsableNOS` (+0x90) from the weakest nitrous among `VEHICLE_PLAYERS` (`NosTor`), which `GetPotentialNOS` 0x407BE0 (`this` = the action, `fld [esp+4]; push esi; fcomp` prologue) checks first: no human nitrous = no AI nitrous. "Allowed" calls it with `mUsableNOS` = 1 and a positive `mNosCapability` and puts the old values back.

- Development logging of `nfsmw_improved_ai_opponents` is behind `[debug] log=1` of its ini: a line per route search (`ai search #n`: where it starts, how many steps the last one took), a line per road segment of a new route (width, lane types, flags, turn), the biggest extra costs of the route search.

## Editing the road network live, and a free camera [read in the exe and the decomp; not run]
- **The arrays are the track's own data, not copies**: `WRoadNetwork::Init` 0x789980 (a sim subsystem: runs when a race loads) points `fProfiles` / `fNodes` / `fSegments` / `fRoads` ([0x9B38B8..0x9B38C8]) straight into the loaded map group (`RN` / `pf nd sg rd`), then runs `ResolveBarriers`, `ResolveShortcuts`, `ResetRaceSegments`. Changing a node's position, a segment's flags or handles, or a profile's lanes in place therefore takes effect on the next read; counts (`fNum*` at 0x9B3870..0x9B3884) and the pointers are plain statics, so bigger arrays could be swapped in. Whether the memory is writable was not checked (heap data from the track load, so it should be).
- **What else caches the network**: (1) `WGrid` (`WGrid::Get().FindNodes`, elements of type `WGrid_kRoadSegment`): the spatial index `WRoadNav::FindClosestSegmentInd` (so `InitAtPoint`, shortcut and barrier resolution) searches 32 m around a point; a segment moved far from its grid cell, or a new one, is not found until the grid lists it. (2) Every `WRoadNav` keeps its segment, spline and cookie trail; they refresh as it moves or re-plans (F9), not when the data changes. (3) Barrier and shortcut flags are derived (`ResolveBarriers` / `ResolveShortcuts` can be called again by hand: 0x7889D0 / 0x788890; `ResetBarriers` 0x7653B0, `ResetShortcuts` 0x7652F0).

- **Segment curves** [read in the exe; confirmed against `WRoadSegment::GetStartControl` 0x404390 / `GetEndControl` 0x7647E0 and `BuildSegmentSpline` 0x765770]: a curved segment (flag 0x100) is a cubic Bezier of (node 0, node 0 + start control, node 1 + end control, node 1), control = handle length (ushort, +0xE start / +0xC end) * 500 / 65535 / 127 * the handle's three signed bytes (+0x13 start / +0x10 end); the end scale is a static set at start-up (0x88D540 writes 0.0076295 into 0x9B3950, so the file's value of 6e-6 is not it). A segment's left and right sides (and the nav's corridor limits) are Beziers from the node position + the end's right vector * the offset at each end (`GetStartRightVec` (z, 0, -x) / 127 and `GetEndRightVec` (-z, 0, x) / 127 of that end's handle), with the same control vectors scaled by (side length / node distance): not the middle curve moved sideways, which folds over on a tight bend (`segment_draw::add_corridor` does it the game's way).
- **Which segment a nav is on** [read in the decomp, `WRoadNav::FindClosestSegmentInd`; not yet seen in the game]: `InitAtPoint` (a nav that is reset or off its path) takes the segments of the `WGrid` cells within 32 m and scores each one by its distance to the car, where a point inside the road's profile width (the lanes from the first to the last, at the nearest point) counts as distance 0, plus `dirWeight * (1 - |dot(car heading, segment direction)|) * 10` (not the absolute dot on a one-way segment); the lowest wins, the lowest index on a tie. So where wide segments overlap (the 35.7 m highway profile 193 reaches across both carriageways and the median), the nav's segment is the one best aligned with the car's heading, not the one under it, and the route search starts from that segment: `AStarSearch` seeds with the node of the nav's segment at its current end, and, for every path type but the race route, also the other end of it. The road inspector marks the segment a player's AI nav is on in magenta (`WRoadNav` +0x8E) and logs when it changes.
- **What a lane's type does** [read; the numbers are the PC's `drivable_lanes` / `selectable_lanes` tables]: a lane's type is an `ELaneType` value (0 racing, 1 traffic, 2 drag, 3 cop, 4 cop reckless, 5 reset, 6 starting grid, 7 any), a nav has a lane type (racing by default) and `drivable_lanes[lane type]` is the bit mask of lane types it may drive on (racing: all but 7 and 13; traffic: only type 1), `selectable_lanes` (racing 0x402: types 1 and 10) the lanes whose centre it may pick as its lane offset. `SetBoundPos` takes the lane nearest to the nav's lane offset that is drivable and widens over the drivable neighbours, so a racing nav's corridor on a profile such as 193 (1,1,1,2,1,1,1,2: traffic lanes, a 4 m type 2 lane in the middle, a 7.8 m type 2 shoulder) is the whole 35.7 m, median and both carriageways included. Nothing that stands in a drivable lane (pillars) is in the way as far as the corridor is concerned.
- **The race filter keeps the nav on the race route** [read in the decomp and the exe; the three segments at the first circuit's pillars seen in the log: only the right-most has the in-race flag; the switch not yet tried in the game]: `AIPerpVehicle::Update` sets `mDriveToNav->SetRaceFilter(IsRacing())` every update (the byte at nav + 0x51, the store at 0x429496; `WRoadNav::GetRaceFilter` is also false when `fValidRaceFilter` 0x9B3829 is). With the filter on, `FindClosestSegmentInd` (so `InitAtPoint`: the nav's reset, `CheckOffPath`, the search's goal) skips every segment that is not flagged in race, a nav that has no path walks only in-race segments in the race route's direction (`GetNextOffset`, bit 2), `Reverse` does nothing, and a new nav starts in the direction the race route has. The in-race and race-route-forward flags are set when the race loads by `AddRaceSegments` from the path (`kPathRaceRoute`, plain length cost) found through the race's checkpoints. So the nav is put on the race route's segment nearest to the car, never on the road the car is on if that is not part of the route; the route search (`Admissible` ignores the flag) then starts from that segment, and a nav that follows a path follows it whatever its flags. At the circuit's pillar entrance the three approach roads (nodes 3180, 4049 and 2024) join at node 3179; the in-race one, segment 4087 from node 2024 (a road whose lanes are types 3, 10, 10), has the tightest curve (limit 8 m/s, radius 6 m, against 13 and 14 m/s). The per-player setting `follow_race_route` (Options > AI Driving > Follow Race Route, `[player_N]` in the ini, default on) stubs the store at 0x429496 so that the filter stays off for the nav of an Improved player when it is off.
- **Where a nav lands** [read in the decomp and the exe; the stand-in written, not yet run]: `WRoadNav::FindClosestSegmentInd` 0x785A00 (thiscall: point, direction, direction weight, closest point out, time out; ret 0x14; callers `InitAtPoint` 0x7862F2 and two at 0x788AC9 / 0x788BBA, the shortcut and barrier resolution) picks a segment from the `WGrid` cells within 32 m by the distance to the road (0 inside its profile width, for a direction weight above 0) plus the weight * 10 * (1 - |dot|); nothing says which of two roads side by side the car is on, and a wall between them is not known to the road network. `nav_landing.cpp` hooks it for a point within 6 m of an Improved player's car: the segment the car is on is the one with the least distance outside its road (3D, so an overpass is not the road below), and the game's answer is kept only when it is within `kMaxHops` (4) segments of that one (hops: to a segment that shares a node, ignoring one-way); otherwise the best segment within reach, scored as the game scores them and honouring the nav's race filter (+0x51), is returned with its nearest point on the Bezier. A segment outside the race is never kept either: **the race's area** (`race_area.cpp`) is every segment that can be reached from the segments flagged in race by hops over shared nodes without using a segment that crosses a track barrier (bits 12, 13, `ResolveBarriers`; the route search never takes those: `Admissible`), worked out when the network or the route changes; with no race (no in-race segment, `fValidRaceFilter` false) everything counts as inside. The road inspector draws segments and nodes outside it dark blue-grey and says so in their label and the log. The F8 overlay's `mark_reachable` is the same idea anchored on the car instead of the route. **The race's direction** (`race_area::route_direction`): a segment of the route has one (bit 2, `RaceRouteForward`: the route runs node 0 to node 1), any other segment gets the direction of the nearest route segment (distance from its middle, 3D, within 60 m); the improved AI's route search charges `kWrongWayWeight` (20) times its length extra for a segment run against it (dot of the step's direction and the race's below -0.5), for a car with Follow Race Route off, which has lost the game's own direction rules [not yet run].
- **Rough lanes and the road itself** [read; seen in the logs: types 1, 2, 3, 7, 10]: a racing nav may drive on every lane type but 7 and 13 (`drivable_lanes[0]` 0x8B5AA0 = 0xFFFFDF7F) but picks its own lane from types 1 and 10 (`selectable_lanes[0]` 0x8B5AC0 = 0x402); `SetBoundPos` 0x76F4A0 builds the corridor from the drivable lane nearest to that lane and widens it over every drivable neighbour, so the game's corridor takes in type 2 as well (sidewalks, medians, shoulders, seen from the editor; drivable but rough: a car cuts a corner over them, and that is allowed). Type 3 and 10 appear on normal roads (the race road at the pillars has 3, 10, 10). Our own numbers count the road (`road::kRoadLanes`: every drivable lane but type 2): `road::road_width` for the A* width term, and the limit speed's band is the widest run of road lanes plus 1.5 m of the rough lane beside it at each side. The game's steering corridor is unchanged (an attempt to take type 2 out of it was wrong: the lanes are drivable, only not preferred). Colours in the editor: green the road, yellow type 2, red 7 and 13 (lanes that lead into obstacles such as the pillar rows: the AI is kept out of them by the drivable mask, though a car could drive there). A segment between profiles with different numbers of lanes (node 1964: 2 lanes of type 10, an 8 m path; node 1979: 6 lanes, 19 m: segment 2048, a shortcut road that joins the highway) has no lane pairing: the game makes each end's corridor limits on their own (`SetBoundPos`) and joins them by a spline, the editor cuts the span at both ends' lane edges at the same share across.
- **Road inspector** (`improved_ai_opponents/src/road_inspector.cpp`, F11; it only shows, the game's road data turned out to be correct so there is no editing) [written; the first version run once]: the camera's position is not asked of the game but solved from the view's WorldClipMatrix (the point whose clip x, y and w are all 0: `Lines::eye`). Hover = what is under the cross in the middle of the picture (the free camera uses the mouse for looking): what is hovered is the nearest to the camera of the candidates inside a wide cylinder around the line through the middle (nodes: within 4 m of it and within 5 % of the picture's height of the cross; only if there is no node, the active node's segments within that many pixels); the left mouse button makes a hovered node the active one (again or on nothing: none). Labels (node number; segment number, length, limit speed, radius) are drawn as lines with a built-in 5 x 7 font (`lines.cpp`) over a blended rectangle, in pixels, without depth. A node's fields as read: position +0, profile +0xE (short), segment count +0x10, segment indices +0x12. The arrays and counts are NOT cleared when a race ends (`WRoadNetwork::Shutdown` frees the memory and nulls only `fgRoadNetwork` 0x9B38B4; `fValid` 0x9B3828): reading them then crashed the game on the way back to the main menu [tested], so `road::loaded()` guards every read. The segments are coloured by `geometry::corner_limit` (`road_geometry.cpp`): the horizontal curvature, averaged over 12 m, of the flattest line through the segment, limit speed = min(top speed, sqrt(lateral_accel * radius)). The line is found at 25 points an equal distance apart along the middle curve: their offsets across the road (along the right vector) that make the sideways part of the second differences smallest (a least squares problem solved directly, with an offset held at the band's edge when it would leave it and the rest solved again; the ends' headings are free, so a wide road that bends gently is a straight line: the funnel segment 5694 came out at 60 m/s, the middle curve's radius is shown as well when it is a lot tighter). The band is a run of lanes at each end (every lane a racing car may drive on, the rough lanes (type 2) between road lanes included, an outer rough lane for 1.5 m only; lanes 7 and 13 end a run; the pair of runs whose middles are nearest is joined), less 2.5 m at each edge. A first version that pulled a polyline tight by relaxation did not converge (more passes made it worse); equal spacing matters: points unevenly spaced along a straight line have second differences too. [step 1 of a travel-time route cost: the numbers are only shown so far; the formula checked on a quarter circle of radius 100 (curvature 0.0098 to 0.0101)]. When it is turned on, the log lists segments over 300 m or with a missing node (looking for the corridors that run off to one distant point in the F8 view).
- **The game already has a per-car cornering model** [read in the decomp, `AIActionRace.cpp` / `AIVehicle.cpp`; PC offsets not checked]: each `AIVehicle` computes its top speed and an acceleration table by speed (`Physics::Info::ComputeAccelerationTable`, `mTopSpeed` / `mAccelData`, decomp offset 0x750) from its attributes. `GripTor`: start grip = the smaller of the tires' static grip (front, rear) in g, end grip = start grip * (gravity - downforce at top speed / mass) / gravity, and for a race the minimum over the human players' cars. `GetSpeedLimit(curvature, f0, f1, top_speed)` = (g*f1 + sqrt((g*f1)^2 + 4*g*f0*|curvature|)) / (2*|curvature|), capped by top speed: the speed at which a car with grip f0 + f1 * speed (in g) can hold that curvature; `AIActionRace::GetPotentialSpeed` uses it on the cookie trail's curvature (and the skill) to set the speed the car wants. The route search does not use any of it. Used by the route search since `car_stats.cpp`: top speed and acceleration through `IVehicleAI::GetTopSpeed` / `GetAcceleration` (vtable slots 29 and 30 of the IVehicleAI interface; the vtable at 0x891A14 holds them in `AIVehicle`'s case), the grip from the tires attributes (`SuspensionRacer` + 0xA0, `STATIC_GRIP` at +0x38 front, +0x3C rear, in g x 9.8), braking taken equal to the grip (the game's AI has no braking model); the downforce term (`EndGrip`) is left out.
